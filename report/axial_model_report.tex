\author{Axial Modeling Project}

\begin{abstract}
Seafloor uplift at Axial Seamount records reservoir deformation, whereas
the pressure and stress that drive rupture remain hidden. We use processed
Center bottom-pressure-recorder (BPR) observations and a three-dimensional
PyLith model to relate the 1998--2011, 2011--2015, and post-2015 uplift
histories to reservoir loading.
The geometry, thermal and viscosity laws, and joint tensile--shear failure
test follow Cabaniss et al. (2020) where their inputs are specified. Five
cases use elastic, uniform Maxwell, temperature-dependent Maxwell,
hydrothermal Maxwell, and tectonic-plus-hydrothermal loading. We fit
deployment-relative uplift without published model outputs. On the 400 m
mesh, the elastic screen reaches joint failure in November 2012, before the
observed April 2015 eruption. Its 1998--2011 crossing lies in an unobserved
BPR gap. The uniform Maxwell case crosses in that gap only under negative
fitted pressure; the other Maxwell cases do not reach joint failure at either
observed eruption. Post-2015 BPR loading crosses the elastic screen in
January 2017, while no Maxwell case crosses through October 2026.
These are conditional stress screens because BPR datums, the basal boundary,
Maxwell spectrum, and mechanical time step remain unresolved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Physical question and observations}
Reservoir inflation deforms the seafloor and changes stress around a magma
chamber. A BPR depth series constrains vertical motion; it does not directly
measure magma pressure or rock failure. Cabaniss et al.\ \cite{cabaniss2020}
connected the Axial record to these hidden quantities with finite elements.
We reconstruct that sequence in PyLith to ask whether observed uplift loads
a cavity and connected crustal shear path to failure.

The input observations are processed Center records in the archives cited by
Cabaniss et al.\ \cite{chadwick,fox}. We select actual hourly values at a
seven-day grid from 13 April 2011 to 22 April 2015, before the 24 April
eruption. The 1998--2011 reconstruction uses the same archived Center
deployments and has a 708-day observational gap after 1998. Each deployment
has an unresolved vertical datum. For pressure
inference, its first selected depth defines zero uplift separately; we do
not use the visual splice in the fit. Inferred pressure is relative to
13 April 2011 rather than absolute reservoir pressure.

For the current monitoring interval we use the Ocean Observatories Initiative
(OOI) Central Caldera MJ03F daily mean seafloor-depth product \cite{ooi}.
The product is detided and drift-corrected by OOI. Its deepest observed day
within 25 April--8 May 2015, 4 May, defines zero relative uplift. The public
record used here contains 3,728 daily values through 17 October 2026 and
457 missing days; all returned daily-depth quality flags are 2, meaning
``not evaluated.'' No observation is filled across a gap.

Figures 1--5 follow the topics and order of Figures 1--5 in Cabaniss et al.,
but show our available observations and model states. The first two figures
establish the observed setting and BPR input; Figures 3--5 show our
material fields and mechanical calculations.

\section{Model and inference}
The cavity is a 6 by 3 by 1 km ellipsoid centered 1.6 km below the seafloor
and oriented N30$^\circ$W, following Cabaniss et al.\ \cite{cabaniss2020}.
The 50 by 50 by 10 km domain and fixed base are implementation choices;
the source specifies a Winkler foundation but omits domain dimensions.
Lateral boundaries have zero normal motion. The 400 m near-cavity mesh has
8,192 tetrahedra and a 4.32\% cavity-volume deficit relative to the ideal
ellipsoid. PyLith pressure increments are added to gravity-equilibrated
reference stress. Four cases omit cycle-long tectonic loading. A separate
case imposes 60 mm\,yr$^{-1}$ full N60$^\circ$E extension across the 68.3 km
projected box width, then removes its modeled uplift before fitting pressure.
The azimuth is our approximation of ridge-normal spreading. The paper's main
text states the full rate, whereas its Table S1 lists boundary velocities of
$\pm20$ mm\,yr$^{-1}$. Initial viscous strain is zero.

Figure 3 of Cabaniss et al.\ labels the uniform Maxwell viscosity
$10^{16}$ Pa\,s. Its uniform modulus color suggests 60 GPa, which Table S1
gives for an analytical solution; the FEM value is not separately tabulated.
The elastic reference uses the project's existing 50 GPa model. Thermal
cases use the printed supplementary laws
\begin{align}
 \eta(T)&=A_D\exp\left(\frac{E_A}{R_gT}\right),&
 E(T)&=E_D+\frac{E_B}{1+C_S\exp\{A_S(1-T/T_{\max})\}} .
\end{align}
Viscosity uses kelvin, $A_D=10^9$ Pa\,s, $E_A=1.2\times10^5$
J\,mol$^{-1}$, and $R_g=8.3114$ J\,mol$^{-1}$\,K$^{-1}$.
For modulus, $E_D=25$ GPa, $E_B=50$ GPa, $A_S=12$, $C_S=5$,
and $T_{\max}=1200^\circ$C.
The cavity is at 1200$^\circ$C, external geotherm at
30$^\circ$C\,km$^{-1}$, internal heat production at zero, and
$k_0=3$ W\,m$^{-1}$\,K$^{-1}$. The hydrothermal conductivity law
uses Nu$=8$, $A=0.75$, $T_{\max}=600^\circ$C, and $z_{\max}=6$ km.
The printed modulus law increases with temperature, contrary to the source's
brittle/ductile labels and property-map colors. We retain the literal law.

PyLith uses one Maxwell branch at these absolute viscosities. The source
describes a generalized Maxwell body but does not publish branch weights
or relaxation times. A unit pressure ramp gives each case's surface response;
we fit pressure changes to deployment-relative BPR motion and run the
inferred history forward. The fit penalizes squared second differences of
weekly pressure at weight 1; weights 0.1 and 10 test gap sensitivity.
These are reconstruction choices. No published pressure, volume, stress,
or failure output enters the fit.

Joint failure requires positive maximum principal stress at the reservoir
for zero tensile strength and a face-connected Mohr--Coulomb shear-yield path
from cavity to seafloor. The shear screen uses source values of 1 MPa
cohesion and 25$^\circ$ friction angle. Zero pore pressure and tensile
cutoffs of 0.1 and 2.5 MPa are additional assumptions.

\section{Failure and numerical limits}
The elastic 2011--2015 history first meets the cavity-tension and connected
shear-path screens on 7 November 2012 at 13.09 MPa. Its seven-cell path on
the 400 m mesh begins on a reservoir flank. The 1998--2011 elastic fit also
crosses, on 19 January 1999, within a long gap in the archived BPR record.
The uniform Maxwell fit crosses formally in that same gap, but under a
negative pressure change; that crossing is not an overpressure-triggered
eruption prediction. The three Maxwell fits for 2011--2015 remain below the
joint criterion at the observed eruption date. Figure \ref{fig:failure}
displays those calculations alongside the separate tectonic sensitivity and
post-2015 observed loading.

The uniform case's continuation bracket happens to overlap the 12--14 MPa
eruption overpressure reported for the source's temperature-dependent cases.
It requires multiplying our fitted final pressure by 15.5, and its first
joint state has about 261 MPa cavity tension. That overlap is not agreement
in rheology, deformation history, or eruption timing. The last modeled
first-order cavity-volume increases are 0.02281, 0.04300, and 0.03995 km$^3$
for the uniform, thermal, and hydrothermal cases, respectively.

The hottest cells have a Maxwell time near 1,354 s, compared with the
604,800 s step in the four-year fit. A seven-day, 0--1 MPa PyLith ramp
changes final incremental stress by 6.75\% in the thermal case and 8.41\%
in the hydrothermal case between seven-day and 30-minute steps. Even the
30-minute step exceeds the shortest cell relaxation time by a factor 1.33.
This demonstrates time-step sensitivity, not convergence of the four-year
pressure history. Mechanical mesh convergence also remains open.

\section{Post-2015 observed loading}
Figure \ref{fig:post2015} maps the observed OOI depth changes to the same
PyLith cavity and failure screen. Relative uplift reaches 2.672 m on the
last available day. The elastic stress screen first meets both joint
conditions on 23 January 2017 at an inferred 13.09 MPa pressure change.
The four Maxwell cases, including the separate hydrothermal spreading run,
do not meet both conditions through their last weekly state on
12 October 2026. Their maximum cavity principal stresses over that interval
remain negative: $-13.51$, $-17.01$, $-17.83$, and $-17.94$ MPa for uniform,
thermal, hydrothermal, and spreading plus hydrothermal Maxwell, respectively. Thus the
elastic configuration crosses during continued observed reinflation,
whereas the Maxwell configurations give no crossing date within the
observed window. These dates are retrospective BPR-constrained failure
screens, not forecasts of a future eruption.

\section{Interpretation}
The model maps BPR deformation to pressure, cavity volume, and stress
without importing the paper's output data. It does not reproduce the
paper's modeled eruption state: the elastic threshold is reached in 2012,
and the Maxwell cases remain below joint failure in April 2015. The mismatch
could reflect the single
Maxwell branch, initial state, thermal modulus conflict, basal treatment,
observation datums, or resolution. The published numerical inputs do not
isolate these effects. A defensible threshold requires constrained gaps
and initial state, a resolved Maxwell spectrum and base response, and
converged cavity stress and path connectivity. The 1998--2011 cycle and a
ridge-normal tectonic sensitivity are included here; neither resolves the
mismatch at both eruption dates. Comparing 250 m and 400 m cavity meshes
will test whether first failure time and path location depend on resolution.
We do not draw a Figure 6-style eruption pathway: no fitted rheology reaches
the joint failure condition at the observed eruption time.

\section{Reproducible record}
The BPR, failure-threshold, and figure targets documented in the repository
README regenerate the histories and figures from cited BPR inputs and
PyLith 5.0.2. Local summary files
record source and output digests. Raw observations, meshes, and PyLith
outputs are untracked; the source audit and parameter record state the
remaining assumptions. The six figures here use cited observations,
model inputs, or generated results; none copies or digitizes a published
panel.
